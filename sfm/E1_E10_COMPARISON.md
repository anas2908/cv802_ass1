**What “higher quality” changes:** native-resolution still photos; `first_octave=-1`, lower SIFT peak threshold `0.004`, affine-shape estimation, domain-size pooling with 10 scales, and features extracted around the person with 32-pixel padding. Spatial selection caps features at 18,000 per image. All E3–E10 matching experiments use those same saved features. Video frames remain 1080×1920 throughout.

**Pair selection and guided matching are different:** pair selection chooses which two images to compare. Guided matching uses their verified geometry to search for additional feature correspondences. “ON for every pair” in E10 means every pair is offered the guided pipeline; a pair must first have suitable geometry before guided refinement can succeed. Unique exhaustive pairs are `N × (N−1) / 2`, not `N × N`.

E10 changes both pair selection and guided coverage relative to E3/E4, so it does not isolate their individual effects. It reuses extracted features; its runtime excludes their extraction. For light shirt, E1 averages 7,529 features per image and E10 averages 13,933. More features plus guided refinement make each pair more expensive even when both methods check all 7,750 pairs.

The Mac has an Apple M5 Pro with 15 physical CPU cores and 24 GB of RAM. At the user's request E10 changed from one to two matching workers after 1,152 pairs were durably saved, then tried four workers after 1,920 saved pairs. The completed guided results were verified and inherited unchanged. Original checkpoints and migration records are retained. A memory monitor can return the run to two workers if four cause sustained pressure; that fallback uses batch129 to preserve each checkpoint's independent execution recipe. Feature quality, pair coverage and matching thresholds stay the same. E10 has a mixed-worker runtime, not a benchmark of a single worker setting.

**Selective pairing in E3:** combines nearby camera directions, shared reconstructed person points from E1, cross-capture links and chronological neighbours. It retains every input image. E3–E10 reuse E1 camera poses, so this tests matching and sparse-point refinement, not a fresh camera-estimation method for each experiment.

**Vocabulary-tree comparison:** E8 uses genuine retrieved pairs only. Its guided subset uses the same geometric priorities and pair budget as E3, restricted to those retrieved pairs. E8 checks more ordinary pairs than E3, so this comparison does not hold the matching workload constant.

**90% versus 97%:** a point must project inside the person mask in at least that fraction of all usable camera views, pooling the views across capture groups. Views where its projection is outside the image or behind the camera do not vote. The body-mask rule also requires at least 25 positive views for light or 58 for dark, plus a track seen in at least three distinct images. E4, E7 and E9 use identical mask files, filter settings and per-subject filter scripts; E10 uses that same policy. Dark crutch points can instead pass the separately reviewed protection rule. These percentages describe mask agreement, not reconstruction accuracy or the percentage of points kept. The 97% rule removes more points, including some real legs/shoes affected by mask errors; 90% preserved a better visual balance in these captures.

Point totals refer to the view named in the “Cleanup shown” column; E1 includes background and should not be ranked against cleaned maps just by counting points.
